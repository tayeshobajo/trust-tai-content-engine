# THE TRUST TAI WORLD BIBLE

## Canon Version 1.0 — The World of Living Roads

**Status:** Foundational canon
**Purpose:** Preserve the thinking, philosophy, visual grammar, and generative method that produced the first two definitive Trust Tai world scenes.
**Governing principle:** The world must remain recognizable through what it believes—not merely through what it looks like.

---

## 1. The Foundational Declaration

This is Tai's world.

It is a vast, lived-in civilization where the realities people carry internally—weight, purpose, memory, confusion, possibility, direction, responsibility—can become physically visible.

The world does not exist to make ordinary people look small. It creates **awe with belonging**: the environment is larger than any one person, yet every person has a meaningful place within it.

The central drama is not good against evil. It is:

- sight against limited perspective;
- intentional direction against endless movement;
- hidden systems against visible symptoms;
- inherited wisdom against isolated striving;
- human dignity against reduction;
- possibility against the belief that the present is all there is.

The Trust Tai world asks:

> What are they carrying, what can they not yet see, and what becomes possible when the larger map is revealed?

---

## 2. The Emotional Promise

Every scene should move toward one compound emotional destination:

# Awe with belonging

The viewer should feel:

1. **Recognition** — "Something in this image understands what I carry."
2. **Curiosity** — "What are the rules of this place?"
3. **Awe** — "The world is far larger than I imagined."
4. **Belonging** — "I am not outside this possibility. There is a place for me here."
5. **Agency** — "There is a meaningful move I can make."

Beauty alone is not enough. Spectacle alone is failure. The image succeeds when its scale enlarges the viewer's inner world without diminishing the human being inside it.

---

## 3. The Philosophical Spine

### Spirit First

People are seen before they are evaluated.

- Every character has dignity, interiority, history, and agency.
- Their burden is not used as decoration.
- Wisdom is offered through recognition, not superiority.
- The guide does not rescue the protagonist or become the hero.
- Grace and accountability can occupy the same scene.
- Black characters are portrayed with realism, specificity, intelligence, tenderness, and range.

### Roadmap Thinking

The visible situation is rarely the whole situation.

- Point A exists, whether or not the character understands it.
- Point B is not merely a destination; it is a larger state of possibility.
- Movement and progress are different phenomena.
- Perspective changes what can be known.
- Systems, dependencies, and paths become visible from the right elevation.
- The decisive move often reorganizes many later moves.
- A map does not walk the road for someone. It restores choice.

### The Audience Is the Hero

Tai's presence may be felt through the world's intelligence, but he does not need to appear at the center of every image. The central person should usually be someone the audience can inhabit.

Guides, elders, mapmakers, witnesses, and instruments help people see. The protagonist chooses what to do with that sight.

## 6. Symbol System

| Symbol | Canonical meaning | Must never become |
|---|---|---|
| Eagle | altitude, witness, perspective unavailable from the ground | automatic logo, mascot, savior |
| Map | record of relationships and possibilities | generic treasure map or motivational prop |
| Living road | intention, dependency, movement made legible | random glowing line |
| Brass | deliberate human intervention, knowledge shaped by hands | universal gold decoration |
| Glass | visibility with fragility | generic sci-fi screen |
| Stone | history, burden, memory, enduring structure | meaningless floating debris |
| Water/reflection | another layer of truth, memory, or possible self-recognition | decorative puddle |
| Case/container | world, system, memory, or responsibility someone transports | convenient steampunk luggage |
| Door/threshold | entry into a larger reality that still requires consent | obvious portal cliché |
| Light | recognition or active relationship | holiness, goodness, or constant magic glow |
| Height | access to systemic perspective | superiority or moral rank |
| Market | exchange of knowledge, tools, histories, and burdens | exotic visual clutter |

---

## 7. Anti-Drift Rules

Reject or rebuild the scene if:
- it is beautiful but cannot state its human truth
- Tai or a guide becomes the savior
- Black identity feels applied rather than foundational
- the character exists only to demonstrate a metaphor
- the world is epic but emotionally empty
- a symbol appears because it is "on brand" rather than meaningful
- every surface glows
- the scene explains the message like an advertisement
- the image could be published by any innovation consultancy

---

## 8. Scene Approval Test

Every scene must answer YES to all Soul questions:
- Is a human being seen before being evaluated?
- Is their dignity intact?
- Does the scene tell the truth without making them small?
- Does the character retain the decisive choice?
- Is the guide a revealer rather than a rescuer?

---

## 9. The Generative Engine (Step by Step)

Step 1 — Find the human truth: "A person is trying to ________, but they cannot see that ________."
Step 2 — Choose one world law
Step 3 — Give the protagonist ordinary intent
Step 4 — Create the recognition device (reveals, does not solve)
Step 5 — Preserve the choice (stop before the lesson is packaged)
Step 6 — Build the frame in layers (human anchor → physical contradiction → systemic reveal → evidence of life → residue)
Step 7 — Remove one-third of the magic

---

## 10. Final Governing Standard

> What did we help the audience see, why could they not see it before, and what becomes possible now?

> Is the world larger because this scene exists—or did we merely add another beautiful image?

Only the first belongs in canon.
