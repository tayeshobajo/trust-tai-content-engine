# Trust Tai Studio Intelligence Architecture

**Status:** Foundational product doctrine  
**Source:** Tai's Studio intelligence brief, 2026-08-01  
**Relationship to V1:** Extends `BRIEF.md` and `DESIGN.md`. V1 is the production workflow. This document defines the learning system that workflow must grow into.

## Governing Question

Before anything leaves Trust Tai Studio, Studio should be able to answer:

> What did we help the audience see, why could they not see it before, and what becomes possible now?

If it cannot answer that clearly, the work is not ready, even if it looks beautiful.

## Final Operating Principle

Learn Tai's worldview deeply enough to protect its soul. Learn his taste carefully enough to recognize quality. Remain imaginative enough to show him something he would not have conceived alone.

## What Studio Is Becoming

Studio is not a content generator that remembers edits.

Studio is a creative intelligence that gradually understands Tai's taste, worldview, storytelling instincts, and standards while still surprising him.

The core review question is not only:

> Is this good?

It is:

> Is this true to Tai's world, is it worthy of the audience's attention, and what should this production teach us about the next one?

## The Creative Learning Loop

```text
Observe patterns
  -> Suggest possibility
  -> Create story
  -> Tai corrects or approves
  -> Studio interprets the reason
  -> Creative memory updates with evidence and scope
  -> Observe patterns again
```

The heart of the loop is reason, not mimicry.

If Tai removes an eagle, Studio must not learn "Tai does not like eagles." It should test possible reasons:

- The symbol was too literal.
- The eagle made Tai the hero.
- It felt like branding instead of storytelling.
- The metaphor had already been communicated.
- The scene needed greater subtlety.

The learning object is the principle behind the correction.

## Five Intelligence Layers

Every production should strengthen five layers of intelligence.

| Layer | What Studio Learns |
| --- | --- |
| World intelligence | The laws, themes, symbols, characters, emotional promise, and canon of the Trust Tai world. |
| Voice intelligence | How Tai thinks, writes, challenges assumptions, and invites people forward. |
| Story intelligence | Which premises, reveals, characters, structures, and emotional movements create memorable films. |
| Taste intelligence | What Tai repeatedly approves, rejects, rewrites, calls too obvious, or calls unexpectedly right. |
| Audience intelligence | What people respond to, remember, discuss, share, misunderstand, or carry forward. |

## Question Banks By Stage

The full system should carry question banks across these stages. They are not static survey questions. They are diagnostic prompts Studio uses to inspect, score, recommend, and learn.

### Thought Intake

Purpose: decide whether a thought contains a story worth developing.

Question families:

- Human truth: what happened, what Tai noticed, who carries the emotional weight, what contradiction matters, what remains unresolved, and whether the audience can recognize themselves without being directly described.
- Roadmap Thinking: visible problem, hidden system, Point A and Point B, current position versus believed position, actual progress versus movement, dependencies, and what should not be acted on yet.
- Spirit First: private weight, dignity, generous interpretation, accountability, grace, agency, and whether the person is seen before being evaluated.
- Unconventional potential: expected version, hidden mechanism, familiar object behaving by an unfamiliar law, invisible system becoming physical, and images that stop people before they know why.

### Topic Recommendation

Purpose: surface ideas from the larger body of work instead of filling a publishing slot.

Every recommendation should contain:

- Observed pattern.
- Source material.
- Why the idea matters now.
- Human truth.
- Unconventional angle.
- Roadmap connection.
- Audience shift.
- Three possible story premises.
- Similar previous work.
- How this direction remains distinct.
- Confidence level.
- What Studio may be misunderstanding.

### Story Creation

Purpose: prove the truth can become drama.

Question families:

- Story engine: subject, want, visible obstacle, private weight, first attempt, failure, price of remaining unchanged, available new perspective, decisive choice, final image, and unresolved residue.
- Character quality: private detail, false belief, overextended strength, agency, transformation, dignity, and whether the character could return with more to reveal.
- World design: world law, relationship to Spirit First and Roadmap Thinking, object carrying memory, environment responding to choice, elevation, weight, truth changing light/gravity/distance/time/architecture, and what remains unexplained.
- Reveal: what the audience believes at opening, suspects halfway through, understands at reveal, and whether perspective changed rather than information merely being withheld.
- Emotional movement: first five seconds, curiosity, concern, recognition, awe, belonging, hope, residue, and what someone might tell another person.

### World Spine QA

Purpose: prevent beautiful emptiness.

Tests:

- Spirit First test: dignity, unseen weight, compassion with honesty, responsibility, invitation instead of command, and love in the construction of truth.
- Roadmap Thinking test: legible Point A and Point B, visible problem versus underlying cause, gained perspective, intentional next move, dependencies, consequences, and path rather than instant arrival.
- Awe with belonging test: world larger than the character while the character still matters, scale serving meaning, beauty serving truth, and the viewer able to locate themselves inside the metaphor.
- Originality test: not advice wearing cinematic clothing, not a metaphor common online, not something another consultancy could plausibly publish, and one unforgettable image.

### Post QA

Purpose: approve writing that carries Tai's worldview without performing intelligence.

Core checks:

- Opening creates earned curiosity.
- The post reveals something rather than announcing an opinion.
- The deeper truth is specific enough to disagree with.
- Tai speaks from conviction, not performance.
- Every paragraph advances the audience shift.
- Business terminology yields to human language where stronger.
- The ending opens a door rather than summarizes.
- The audience remains the hero.
- Seriousness is not confused with depth.

### Visual And Cinematic QA

Purpose: approve frames and motion before spending render credits.

Keyframe checks:

- Story understandable from keyframes alone.
- One dominant idea per frame.
- Character, wardrobe, age, features, physical proportions, objects, lighting, and world continuity are locked.
- Visual strangeness has meaning.
- The frame does not resemble generic AI concept art.
- Spectacle, floating objects, and glowing particles are not decoration.
- Black characters are preserved with realism, dignity, and specificity.
- The image remains memorable without relying on the navy and brass palette.

Motion checks:

- Every movement has a story reason.
- Physics are believable inside the world's laws.
- The camera reveals information intentionally.
- Scale, geography, mass, pauses, reveal speed, and emotional settling are preserved.

Sound checks:

- Sound can suggest the hidden mechanism.
- Music does not create emotion the story has not earned.
- Silence can carry weight.
- Narration guides discovery instead of explaining the lesson.
- The final sound creates residue.

### UI Judgment QA

Purpose: make excellent judgment easier.

Command Center should distinguish decisions from background activity and explain why attention is needed.

Thinking Room should show Tai's original thought beside Studio's interpretation and let him correct one inference without rewriting everything.

Approval Desk should let Tai trace paragraphs to approved truth, compare versions by meaning, explain why he dislikes something, and see what Studio learned from revision.

Film Studio should compare concepts on meaning, show risk and cost, allow partial locks, expose continuity rules, and explain what approving a stage authorizes.

Library should show explored ideas, related productions, repeated concepts, correction history, audience feedback, reusable characters, reusable locations, and the difference between continuation and repetition.

Settings and Memory should let Tai see what Studio currently believes, correct learned assumptions, mark preferences as permanent/contextual/experimental, inspect evidence, prevent one unusual production from becoming a global preference, reset patterns, approve world canon, and identify conflicting preferences.

## Correction Learning

Every meaningful correction should open a lightweight learning prompt.

Studio should ask:

- What was wrong with the previous version?
- Was the issue truth, tone, originality, structure, emotion, visual execution, or technical quality?
- Was the idea wrong, or was its expression weak?
- What must remain unchanged?
- Is this correction specific to this production?
- Should this become a preference for future work?
- Does it apply to all Trust Tai content or only this format?
- Is Tai rejecting the direction or asking for stronger execution?
- What principle does the correction reveal?
- How confident should Studio be in applying it elsewhere?

Fast correction labels:

- Too obvious
- Too literal
- Too generic
- Too polished
- Feels AI-generated
- Emotionally cold
- Missing human weight
- Weak reveal
- Wrong truth
- Overexplained
- Too much spectacle
- Not Trust Tai
- Character lacks dignity
- Beautiful but empty
- Strong idea, weak execution
- Keep this, change only
- Unexpectedly right

The last label matters. Studio must learn from positive surprise, not only rejection.

### Learning Confirmation Pattern

After a correction, Studio should state what it thinks it learned.

Example:

> I believe the issue was not the use of the eagle itself. The eagle appeared as a decorative brand symbol instead of revealing a perspective the character could not access. I will preserve eagles as meaningful witnesses, not automatic visual signatures.

Tai can respond:

- Correct
- Partly correct
- Wrong conclusion
- Apply to this production only
- Make this a Studio principle

## Creative Memory Model

Every inferred preference should store:

- What Studio believes.
- Evidence supporting it.
- Number of approvals or corrections reinforcing it.
- Whether it was explicit or inferred.
- Scope: permanent, provisional, contextual, or experimental.
- Formats it applies to.
- Exceptions observed.
- Last reinforced date.
- Conflicts with other preferences.
- Whether Studio should follow it, warn about it, or merely consider it.

Example learned principles:

| Learned Principle | Evidence | Scope | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reveal perspective visually before explaining it. | 7 approved post and film revisions. | All storytelling | High |
| Avoid literal eagle imagery unless altitude changes meaning. | 3 corrections. | Films and featured images | Medium |
| End with possibility, not a packaged moral. | 11 approvals. | Posts and films | High |
| Brass indicates intentional intervention. | Approved world canon. | Trust Tai world | Canon |

High confidence must not become a cage. Studio may break a principle when it can explain why breaking it creates something stronger.

## World Canon Model

When a new character, location, object, symbol, or law appears, Studio should classify:

- Does it already exist?
- Is this a variation or new element?
- What does it mean?
- What does it never mean?
- What are its limitations?
- How does it relate to Spirit First?
- How does it relate to Roadmap Thinking?
- Does it belong to one story, one reality, or the entire world?
- Can characters know about it?
- What happens when its rules are broken?
- Is it canon, provisional, or unique to one story?
- Does it contradict anything established?
- Is it strong enough to recur?
- Should some meaning remain mysterious?

## Story Scoring System

Studio may score drafts internally, but it must not reduce creativity to a number.

Dimensions:

| Dimension | Core Question |
| --- | --- |
| Human truth | Does this reveal something honest about being human? |
| Spirit | Is the person treated with truth, dignity, and care? |
| Perspective | Does the audience see something unavailable at the beginning? |
| Audience shift | Does understanding genuinely change? |
| Originality | Could this only have come from Tai's world? |
| Dramatic strength | Does something meaningful happen? |
| Character agency | Does the character make the decisive move? |
| Visual memory | Is there an image people will remember? |
| Emotional residue | Does the film remain with the viewer? |
| Producibility | Can the idea survive actual execution? |

Rules:

- A story cannot proceed if Human Truth, Spirit, or Audience Shift is weak.
- High visual memory cannot compensate for an empty story.
- Originality must come from the premise, not unusual imagery alone.
- Studio must explain every low score and suggest a repair.
- Tai's approval overrides scoring, but the override becomes learning evidence.

## Post-Publication Learning

Audience response should inform Studio without letting engagement metrics control the brand.

Studio should distinguish:

- Reach
- Recognition
- Resonance
- Conversation
- Trust
- Business relevance

Investigate:

- What people quoted or paraphrased.
- What generated thoughtful comments.
- What viewers misunderstood.
- Whether people discussed the truth or merely praised visuals.
- Which moment was remembered.
- Whether the intended audience recognized themselves.
- Whether the invitation created reflection or merely clicks.
- Which comments revealed a new story opportunity.
- Whether a quiet post created more meaningful conversations than a high-reach post.

## Proactive Intelligence

Studio should periodically surface observations across the body of work.

Examples:

> You have explored founder dependence in four different forms: the tilted office, the organization being dragged behind its founder, the city with one open door, and the railway without a shared destination. The recurring truth appears to be: "Being needed everywhere is not the same as leading."

> Your last six posts reveal hidden systems beneath visible problems. The pattern is strong, but the audience has not yet seen what happens after the correct system is built. I recommend a story about relief, shared intelligence, and regained imagination.

> You have rejected three concepts where the eagle appeared prominently. Your corrections suggest that you value the eagle as a change in perspective, not a recurring character. Should I update the World Bible accordingly?

## Minimum V1 Intelligence Addition

Do not rebuild the app around all 14 areas at once.

The first structural addition should make the loop real:

1. Add a creative memory data model.
2. Capture correction events from Approval Desk and Film Studio.
3. Let Tai label the correction quickly.
4. Ask Studio to propose the principle behind the correction.
5. Store the principle with evidence, scope, confidence, and exceptions.
6. Show learned principles in Settings and on the relevant production.
7. Make Library preserve correction history and related productions.

Suggested first data structures:

```ts
type IntelligenceLayer =
  | "world"
  | "voice"
  | "story"
  | "taste"
  | "audience"

type CorrectionCategory =
  | "truth"

  | "this_production"
```

## First Implementation Slice

Name: Creative Memory Foundation

Acceptance criteria:

- Settings gains a Memory section showing learned principles grouped by World, Voice, Story, Taste, and Audience.
- Each learned principle shows belief, evidence count, scope, confidence, last reinforced date, and behavior.
- Approval Desk and Film Studio expose a lightweight "Teach Studio" action after a hold/revision.
- The prompt supports fast labels, Tai's note, what must remain unchanged, and whether the lesson applies only here or more broadly.
- Studio records correction events locally in V1 without requiring a backend migration.
- Library package panel shows a correction history and "what Studio learned" summary.
- Nothing automatically becomes permanent. Tai must confirm "Make this a Studio principle."

## Product Guardrails

- Do not turn Tai's preferences into a cage.
- Do not let one unusual production become a global rule.
- Do not optimize for reach over recognition, resonance, conversation, trust, and business relevance.
- Do not confuse visual memory with story strength.
- Do not let brand symbols become shortcuts.
- Do not let the system explain away a weak story with beautiful images.
- Do not auto-publish, auto-render, or auto-canonize in V1.
